## Cite Responsibly

- Cite the method or framework that materially shaped the work, not every familiar research practice.
- Phrase lineage as “using common JTBD timeline interviewing” or “using behavior-based persona conventions” unless exact sourced language is available.
- Do not claim that a prompt was authored by, endorsed by, or quoted from a named author.
- For current facts, regulations, platform guidelines, competitor claims, pricing, or benchmarks, use current primary sources and include access dates.
- For sample-size or statistical guidance, state the assumptions and calculation. Do not cite a heuristic as universal proof.

## Calibrate Claim Strength

| Evidence | Appropriate wording |
|---|---|
| One source or anecdote | “One participant/source indicated…” |
| Repeated qualitative pattern | “A pattern across X of Y sources suggests…” |
| Contradictory qualitative evidence | “Experiences differed; the available evidence supports at least two contexts…” |
| Non-representative survey | “Among respondents to this survey…” |
| Representative or weighted estimate | State population, sampling, field dates, estimate, and uncertainty |
| Controlled experiment | State assignment, exposure, primary outcome, effect, uncertainty, and analysis caveats |
| No user evidence | “Assumption,” “hypothesis,” or “proto-artifact,” never “finding” |

## Preserve Research Ethics

- Obtain informed consent for participation and recording.
- Collect only data needed for the decision; define retention and access.
- Anonymize direct identifiers and avoid exposing sensitive transcript details.
- Make participation and incentives fair, accessible, and non-coercive.
- Include users with relevant access needs instead of simulating disability as a substitute.
- Escalate regulated, clinical, safety-critical, child-related, or highly sensitive research to qualified research, legal, privacy, or ethics support.
